Who Gained, Who Lost — 2021 Peak vs 2024

The 2021 peak was the COVID bubble. By 2024, the real winners had separated from the pandemic beneficiaries. The total active user base of these 8 apps shifted dramatically — Venmo and Robinhood lost relative share while Klarna and Revolut surged.

Key Moments That Shaped the Race

2018
Cash App overtakes Venmo in downloads
For the first time, Cash App — backed by Jack Dorsey's Square — surpassed Venmo in total cumulative downloads in the US. Venmo had dominated since 2012 but Cash App's simpler interface and Bitcoin integration were pulling a new generation of users away from PayPal's ecosystem.
JAN 2021
GameStop — Robinhood's finest hour and first crisis
Robinhood hit 3 million downloads in January 2021 alone — its highest ever month — as retail traders piled into GameStop and AMC. Then it halted trading, angering millions. It never fully recovered user trust. The meme stock era created Robinhood's peak and planted the seeds of its decline.
2021
Venmo reaches 70M users — and then peaks
Venmo hit 70 million monthly active users in 2021, its all-time high. But PayPal's parent company had largely ignored product innovation, and Cash App was now offering Bitcoin, investing, and a debit card. Venmo's social payment model was no longer enough to compete on its own.
2022
The great fintech correction
Rising interest rates, the crypto crash, and the end of pandemic-era trading decimated the sector. Robinhood's user base fell from 22.5M to 14M. Coinbase's monthly transacting users crashed. Chime's growth stalled. Valuations across fintech dropped 60–90%. It was the industry's first true reckoning.
2023
Revolut doubles in a single year
Revolut added 20 million customers in 2023 — growing from 18M to 38M — making it the fastest-growing major fintech company in the world that year. Its super-app model (banking, FX, crypto, insurance, travel) and aggressive pricing proved the European neobank thesis in a way Monzo and N26 had not yet matched.
2024
Klarna's AI pivot changes the BNPL story
Klarna announced its AI assistant was handling the work of 700 customer service employees. Its app users surged from 40M to 65M in a single year. The company filed for an IPO valued at $15B+. What started as "buy now pay later" had become a full shopping and financial super-app — and AI was at the centre of its efficiency story.
2024
Monzo hits profitability — the neobank model finally works
Monzo reported £1.2 billion in revenue in 2024 and its first full year of profitability, proving that digital-only banks can build sustainable businesses. With 12.1 million customers and an NPS score that traditional banks can only dream of, Monzo became the template for what a challenger bank should look like.
2025
Robinhood's comeback — revenue without users
Robinhood posted $4.5 billion in revenue in 2025 — a record, up 52% — despite having far fewer users than its 2021 peak. The secret: higher-value users, prediction markets, Gold subscriptions, and international expansion. Revenue per user had replaced user count as the metric that mattered. The fintech business model had matured.

What This Means for Finance Professionals

The incumbent risk is real — and closer than you think

In 2018, Venmo and traditional payment apps looked unassailable. By 2024, Klarna and Revolut had matched or exceeded their active users — with a fraction of the history. Every financial institution watching this chart should ask: which of our products could a well-funded challenger replicate in five years, and what would we do about it?

Growth without retention is a trap

Chime signed up 25 million people but only kept 8.7 million active. Robinhood peaked at 22.5 million and fell to under 12 million. The COVID era inflated user counts across fintech, and the correction revealed which companies had built real value versus which had benefited from a once-in-a-generation stimulus event. Sustainable fintech growth requires daily utility — not just a one-time sign-up moment.

AI is not a feature — it's the new cost structure

Klarna's 2024 story is the most important lesson for anyone in financial services. By deploying AI across customer service and credit decisioning, they grew users 63% while cutting operational costs simultaneously. Revolut is applying the same playbook across 38 countries. The fintech companies winning in 2025 are not just growing faster — they are structurally cheaper to run. That is a permanent competitive advantage.
